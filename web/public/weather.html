Will it rain here in the next hour?

Every five minutes this page picks places where the NEXRAD radar network is tracking a storm and asks four forecasters whether it will be raining there 15, 30 and 60 minutes later. When that time comes, the radar shows who was right.

The same model, but moving the rain with the winds 9,000 to 18,000 ft up, from NOAA's winds-aloft forecast.

Baseline: same as now

Predicts that whatever the radar shows now stays exactly as it is. The standard yardstick: a forecast is only useful if it beats this.

NWS forecast

The National Weather Service's official hourly forecast for that spot: its chance of rain for the hour. Counted as calling rain at 50% or more.

Rain means the radar shows an echo of 20 dBZ or more at the spot (about 0.65 mm/h, light rain). The comparison with NWS is indicative: its forecast is the chance of measurable rain at any time during the hour, while the radar check is a single moment.

How it works

The model is examples/weather_nowcast.aven: moisture (Ambaum 2020 saturation curve), hydrostatic density with virtual temperature, a plane fit through three stations' winds for divergence, vorticity and deformation, vertical shear, Marshall-Palmer rain rates, and the nowcasts. avenc emit-wasm compiles it to a 4 KB module, weather.wasm, which the job runs and which your browser can re-run from any stored inputs with the button on each run. Its exp and fractional powers come from the JavaScript host (aven_math.js).

verify/weather_reference.py checks the same model on 150 random atmospheric states against the relational version (evaluations/possible/weather-predict.aven, solved by avenc), MetPy, NumPy and the closed-form formulas; every output agrees within 1e-9 relative.

What the checks do and don't show

  • The data-quality gate is the bounds in weather-predict.aven; a run that breaks one is stored but issues no forecast.
  • Humidity and pressure compare two processing pipelines for the same station report (the NWS observation and the METAR issued at the same time), so they catch decoding and reduction errors, not a faulty sensor. Radar against a rain gauge, and steering wind against radar-tracked storm motion, compare independent measurements.
  • Scores are for single points in the base reflectivity mosaic. Storm-cell sites are chosen where echoes are strong, which favours forecasts of rain; the fixed airports are a control.
  • Lagrangian persistence is a baseline nowcast, not a weather model: it moves today's echoes and cannot grow or decay them.
